EMPORDÀ COSTA BRAVA

Wedding DJs on the
Costa Brava.

A Costa Brava wedding is almost never one night. The estates here are let for whole weekends with everyone sleeping on site, which means you are not planning a party, you are programming three days. The wedding itself usually takes care of itself. It is the welcome dinner and the day after that get remembered, and they are the two that nobody plans until the week before.

You are programming a weekend, not a night

The reason this region works for international couples is that so many of its estates are let on exclusive use with beds included. Everybody arrives on the Friday, everybody stays, nobody drives home. It produces the best atmosphere of any wedding format we work, because by the time the wedding day arrives the guests already know each other.

It also produces three separate events with three separate briefs, and only one of them tends to get planned properly.

The welcome dinner is the hardest and the most undervalued. Its entire job is to let a hundred people who have just met start talking, which means the music has to be present, warm and quiet enough to disappear behind conversation. It is not a warm-up party. Getting it wrong in the loud direction is the single most common mistake at these weddings, because a welcome dinner where nobody can hear each other undoes the exact thing the weekend format exists to create.

The wedding day is the one everyone plans, and it usually goes fine.

The day after is long, hot, daylit and low intensity, usually around a pool, usually with people arriving and leaving in waves across five or six hours. It needs a genuine source of sound covering an outdoor area, and it needs someone deciding what plays, because a phone on a portable speaker in the sun is what everyone remembers and not for good reasons.

What the extra days actually need

None of this requires a full production on the other days, and it should not cost like the wedding night. What it requires is that somebody has thought about it in advance rather than improvising at 20:00 on the Friday.

  • Coverage, not volume. An outdoor terrace or a pool area needs sound spread across it rather than one loud point source at one end, which is the difference between music being everywhere and music being a nuisance at the near tables and inaudible at the far ones.
  • Power and weather. Somewhere to plug in that is not an extension lead across a lawn, and a plan for the equipment if it rains on a day nobody was watching the forecast for.
  • A person, or a properly built playlist. Either is fine. What does not work is assuming a guest will handle it, because that guest then spends their friend's wedding weekend working.
  • Ceremony sound counted separately. An outdoor ceremony needs its own coverage and its own microphones, not a speaker borrowed from the dinner setup.

Why finishing times vary so much here

This region has the widest range of curfews in Catalonia, which is exactly why a general answer is worthless and why you have to ask about your specific venue.

The mechanisms are the standard Catalan ones. The legal night begins at 23:00, with limits measured at the façade of the nearest dwelling. Outdoor amplified music is commonly capped at 23:00 by the municipal ordinance, and that applies on private land as well. A licensed banqueting venue typically closes around 02:30, with an extra half hour on Friday and Saturday nights and the eve of holidays, and a summer extension towards 03:30. A mayor can also reduce those hours by up to two hours, though only exceptionally and with justification such as documented nuisance or security grounds.

What makes the Empordà extreme is the geography. The medieval villages are dense, so a venue inside one is tightly constrained. The coastal towns are built up, so the same applies. But out in the fields between them are estates with no other building within half a kilometre, and when there is no neighbour there is no complaint and therefore nothing to measure. Two venues twenty minutes apart can genuinely have completely different nights available to them.

These are the general mechanisms rather than a ruling on your venue. Hours are set per venue and per municipality and they change from year to year. Confirm the position for your specific date with the venue in writing.

The coast and the interior are different weddings

The coastal Costa Brava is cliffs, pines and small coves: Begur, Llafranc, Tamariu, Aiguablava. Spectacular, exposed to the sea breeze, and generally close to other buildings, which usually means an earlier outdoor stop and a party that has to move inside.

Inland, twenty minutes back from the water, the Baix Empordà is farmland, cork oaks and medieval villages like Pals, Peratallada and Monells, with restored masias scattered between them. This is where the exclusive-use estates are, where the isolation is, and where the late nights happen.

Most couples who want both put the guests near the coast for the weekend and hold the wedding inland. It works well, with the single caveat that it introduces a transfer, and a transfer at 19:00 is a moment where a wedding can lose its momentum if nothing is planned to receive people at the other end.

How we work in the Empordà

For a weekend wedding we would rather cover more of it than less, not to sell hours but because the welcome dinner and the day after are where these weddings are actually won, and because it removes the risk of three suppliers each assuming somebody else brought the ceremony microphones.

Where the estate has an installed system or a technical company already engaged, we come as DJ only. Where it does not, we supply what the spaces need, which for a multi-day booking usually means one sensible outdoor setup that stays put and a proper system for the wedding night. Where you have engaged an AV or production company separately, we work alongside them.

We do not publish prices, because a single evening on a house system and a three-day supplied booking across four different spaces are not remotely the same job. Tell us the estate, the dates and how many of the days you want covered, and you get a real number.

Frequently asked questions

Why are Costa Brava weddings usually more than one day?
Because the estates are let on exclusive use with accommodation, so guests stay for two or three nights. That produces a welcome dinner and a recovery day alongside the wedding itself.

How does music work across three days?
Different jobs. The welcome dinner needs to be quiet enough for conversation, the pool day needs long low-intensity coverage in daylight, and only the wedding night is a party. Plan all three.

What time does the music have to stop?
The widest spread in Catalonia. Legal night at 23:00, outdoor music commonly capped at 23:00, licensed close typically around 02:30 to 03:30, and councils can cut that. Isolated estates are the exception with no practical limit.

Which airport?
Girona is twenty to forty minutes away but seasonal. Barcelona is ninety minutes to two hours and serves long-haul. Name both.

Tell us the estate and the dates, including the days either side. Those are usually the ones worth talking about.
