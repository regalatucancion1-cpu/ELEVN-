Wedding DJ at Masia Victoria

A 15th-century Catalan manor ten minutes from Sitges, and one of the easiest venues in the region to run a proper party in. Here is why, what time the music actually has to stop, and the two things worth settling before you book anyone.

The bodega is the whole story

Most of the anxiety couples bring to a Spanish wedding venue is about noise: how loud can we be, when do they pull the plug, will the neighbours ruin it. At Masia Victoria almost none of that applies, and the reason is architectural rather than legal.

The late-night party happens indoors, in a tall vaulted bodega that links the two patios. Stone walls contain sound. That single fact removes the problem that defines most Catalan wedding venues, and it is worth understanding why.

Catalonia runs two separate rules that couples routinely confuse. The first caps outdoor amplified music, typically at 23:00, and it applies to private installations too, so a masia's own garden speakers are caught by it. The second is the venue's entertainment licence, which governs what time the establishment itself has to close.

A venue where the party is outdoors is bound by the first rule and the night effectively ends at eleven. A venue where the party is indoors is only bound by the second. Masia Victoria is the second kind.

This is why so many Catalan wedding venues are built around an enclosed party room. It is not a stylistic preference. It is the only structure that legally survives past eleven.

What time the music really stops

Every wedding we have played here has run to 3am, and there is a reason that number keeps coming up rather than something arbitrary.

Masia Victoria sits in Sant Pere de Ribes, so it falls under the Catalan closing-hours regime. A venue licensed as a saló de banquets closes at 02:30, with an additional 30 minutes on Saturdays and the eve of a public holiday. That is your 3am. Between 1 June and 15 September a further seasonal extension applies, so a summer Saturday has more room than a Tuesday in March.

Two practical consequences. If your date is a Friday or a Sunday you may have less time than you assumed, so build the running order backwards from the real end time rather than forwards from dinner. And because closing hours are set by licence and can be varied by the town hall, get the end time from the venue in writing for your specific date rather than trusting a figure from a blog, including this one.

Who provides the sound here

This is the question that quietly decides half your music budget, and almost nobody asks it early enough.

At any Spanish wedding, the PA and lighting arrive in one of three ways: the venue has a system installed, the couple hires a separate technical company, or the DJ brings everything. All three are normal. They produce very different quotes and very different evenings.

Masia Victoria works with a catering partner, but music is not exclusive and couples are free to bring their own DJ, band, photographer and everything else. What that means in practice is that the technical side is a decision you get to make rather than one made for you, which is good news as long as somebody makes it.

The failure we see most often at venues like this one is not a missing speaker. It is that the sound company, the caterer and the DJ have never spoken to each other before the day. The rig gets built for a band that is not coming, the booth ends up somewhere the DJ cannot see the floor, the power run is on the wrong side of the room, and it all gets solved at seven in the evening with guests arriving. That is entirely avoidable with one conversation a fortnight out.

Seventy guests is a different kind of night

Masia Victoria is an intimate venue. Around 70 for the wedding, with 14 en-suite bedrooms sleeping roughly 28 on site. That changes how the music should be built, and it is not simply a smaller version of a 200-guest wedding.

  • Everyone is on the floor or nobody is. With 70 people there is no crowd to hide in. Energy has to be led rather than allowed to build on its own.
  • Volume is a tool, not a setting. A stone room this size fills fast. Pushing it does not make the party bigger, it just makes conversation impossible and empties the floor early.
  • Guests sleeping on site changes the last hour. Nobody is watching the clock for a taxi, so the final stretch tends to be the best part of the night if it is paced for it.
  • Live musicians land harder at this scale. A saxophone at 200 guests is a texture. At 70, in a bodega, it is an event.

Before you book anyone

Two things to settle with the venue, in writing, whoever you end up hiring:

  • The music end time for your exact date, indoors and outdoors stated separately. Weekday, Saturday and summer dates are all different.
  • Who is supplying the PA and lighting, and whether that supplier has worked with your DJ or band before. If the answer is no, introduce them.

Neither question is difficult. Both are much easier to answer in February than in the week of the wedding.
