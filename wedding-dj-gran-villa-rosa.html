VILANOVA I LA GELTRÚ GARRAF

Wedding DJ at
Gran Villa Rosa.

Gran Villa Rosa is an exclusive-use estate in the Garraf, seven minutes from Sitges and around twenty-five minutes from Barcelona airport, with a private chapel, an enclosed bodega and rooms on site. This page covers its spaces, how wedding hours work in Catalonia, and how we work there.

Guests on site and guests in town

Part of the guest list sleeps on the estate and the rest stay in Sitges or Vilanova, which is a comfortable arrangement and worth planning the end of the evening around.

One piece of advice that applies anywhere transport is involved: book more than one coach back. Two departures thirty or forty minutes apart let the guests who want an early night take the first while the ones who came to dance stay to the end, rather than everybody leaving at the same moment. It costs less than most couples expect and it makes a noticeable difference to how the last hour feels.

It also means the evening has a natural second stretch for the people staying on site, which is worth treating as its own moment rather than as the same set continuing.

The bodega decides your hours

Catalan rules treat indoor and outdoor music completely differently. Outdoor amplified music is commonly capped at 23:00 by the municipal ordinance, and that applies on private estates too, so being behind your own gates does not exempt you. Noise limits themselves are measured at the façade of the nearest dwelling, not at your dance floor.

An estate with a genuine enclosed bodega therefore has a completely different night available to it than one with only gardens. The garden becomes the setting for the ceremony, the aperitif and dinner. The cellar becomes the party. That is not a compromise, it is the correct use of the building, and it is why a venue like this can run late in a region where a terrace cannot.

Licensed closing hours are set per venue and per municipality and they change from year to year. Confirm the finishing time for your own date with the venue in writing rather than relying on what a previous couple was given.

Playing in the bodega

An enclosed stone cellar is a very good room for a party, and it is one we set up for specifically.

Stone reflects sound, so the approach is to place sources close to the guests rather than firing the length of the room, and to keep deliberate control over the low end so the kick and the bassline stay defined. A room like this also behaves quite differently full than empty, which is worth knowing if anyone is judging it at a quiet afternoon soundcheck. Set up for the room, a bodega sounds full and clear at a level that still lets people talk at the edges.

Twenty-five minutes from the airport

This is a genuinely rare combination and it is worth using. Most exclusive-use estates of this scale are an hour or more from an airport, which costs your guests a day at each end. Here, people can land and be at the estate or at a Sitges hotel very quickly.

Practically, that makes a Friday arrival realistic for guests who cannot take a whole week off, which raises attendance. It also makes a welcome dinner on the Friday viable rather than aspirational, and welcome dinners are the most underrated part of a destination wedding: by the time the wedding day arrives, the guests already know each other, and a room that already knows each other dances hours earlier.

Who supplies the sound

Estates of this kind vary. Some have an installed system and a technical partner already in place, in which case we come as DJ only and work on their rig, which is usually the best outcome for everyone. Some couples contract an AV or production company separately, often through a planner, and we work alongside them. And sometimes the spaces you are using have nothing suitable, in which case we supply the sound and lighting ourselves.

Because this venue also offers entertainment as part of what it can arrange, it is worth asking directly whether an external DJ is welcome rather than assuming it either way. The technical question underneath is simpler and more important: who controls the sound system, and is there a limiter. Those two answers determine whether a visiting DJ arrives with a laptop or with a truck.

Capacities, room figures and accommodation numbers on this page come from information published about the venue rather than from a private quotation, and they change. Confirm the detail that matters to you directly with the estate.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a wedding here have two endings?
Because around forty-five people sleep on site and everyone else shuttles back to the coast. The coach departure is a real break in the night, and the stretch afterwards is a second, smaller party that deserves its own plan.

Indoors or outdoors?
Both exist, and the choice sets your hours. Outdoor music in Catalonia is commonly capped at 23:00 including on private estates, while the enclosed bodega can run to the licensed closing time.

How far is the airport?
Around twenty-five minutes, with Sitges seven minutes away and Barcelona about forty.

Can we bring our own DJ?
Usually yes, but ask directly, since the venue can also arrange entertainment itself. The more important question is who controls the system and whether there is a limiter.
