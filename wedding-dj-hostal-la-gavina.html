S'AGARÓ COSTA BRAVA

Wedding DJ at
Hostal de la Gavina.

Every guide to this hotel will tell you about the gardens, the bay and the Murano chandeliers. Here is the thing that actually shapes your night and that nobody writes down: the people you must not wake up are not neighbours somewhere down the road. They are asleep in rooms above and around your dance floor, and they paid a great deal to be there.

The neighbours are upstairs

At a country estate, the noise question is geographic: how far away is the nearest occupied house, and does anyone live in it. At a hotel that question collapses. The nearest occupied bedroom is directly above the room you are playing in, and its occupant is a paying guest with a reasonable expectation of sleeping.

This matters because it changes who decides. An ordinance is a rule you can read in advance and plan around. A hotel duty manager responding to a complaint at 01:40 is a decision made in real time, and a five-star hotel will protect a sleeping guest ahead of your final hour every single time. That is not a failure of hospitality, it is the business they are in.

What follows from it is practical rather than dramatic. The last part of the night belongs in a room chosen for containment, not for the view. And the thing that actually travels into other rooms is not what most people assume. High frequencies are stopped easily by a closed door. Low frequencies move through the structure of a building, along floors and walls, and arrive upstairs as a thud with no music attached to it, which is far more irritating than the music itself would be.

So the technique at a hotel is specific: keep the sub-bass under control, get the energy from the midrange where the impact lives, and put the level where the guests are dancing rather than filling the whole room with it. Done properly nobody on the dance floor perceives any restraint at all. Done carelessly you get a complaint at the exact moment the wedding was peaking.

Several rooms, and the one decision that matters

The hotel publishes event spaces at very different scales. The Salón Mediterráneo takes around three hundred, Villa d'Este around a hundred and fifty, Las Conchas around a hundred, and there are the Garbí and Bay Bar terraces alongside more than two thousand square metres of gardens.

Couples generally choose these rooms by how dinner will look in them, which is understandable and is the wrong criterion for one of them. The room where the party ends is a different decision from the room where you eat, and it is worth making deliberately.

Two things decide it. Containment, for the reasons above. And proportion: a hundred and twenty guests in a room built for three hundred will feel like a thin wedding no matter what is playing, because a dance floor needs density to work. If your numbers are well under a room's capacity, the smaller room is almost always the better party even though it looks less impressive empty at the viewing.

Outdoors is for daylight

Catalonia caps outdoor amplified music early, commonly at 23:00 under the municipal ordinance, and that applies to private grounds too. At a hotel there is the second and more immediate factor of guests sleeping on site.

So plan the outdoor part of the day as the ceremony, the aperitif and, if you want it, dinner. Terraces above the bay at golden hour are among the best settings on this coast and they should absolutely be used. Just build the evening so the outdoor stretch peaks before it ends, rather than building towards a climax that has to happen somewhere else.

If a hotel ever makes an exception for a particular wedding, treat it as exactly that: their decision, for that day, granted rather than owed. It is not something to design your night around, and any supplier who promises it in advance is promising something that is not theirs to give.

Permitted hours and the use of each space are set by the hotel and by the municipality and they change. Confirm what applies to your own date directly with the hotel, in writing.

The hotel has a sound system. That is not the end of the question

Most good venues have something installed, and it is a fair question why a DJ would arrive with anything else.

The answer is that an installed system and a party system are built for different jobs. Venue installations are usually specified for speech, background music and presentations, spread evenly around a room so that it sounds the same everywhere. That is exactly right for a dinner and exactly wrong for a dance floor, which wants headroom, low-end extension and energy concentrated where people are actually dancing.

Sometimes the house system is genuinely enough, and bringing anything else would be expense and van space for nothing. Sometimes it is not, and discovering that at eleven at night is too late to fix. The honest position is that this is a question with a specific answer per room, and the way to get it is to establish what is installed, where it points and what it is rated for, well before the day.

The same applies to the ceremony. An outdoor ceremony in open gardens with wind off the bay needs its own coverage and its own microphones, and that is very rarely served by whatever exists indoors.

Load-in at a hotel that is still open

An estate closes for your wedding. A hotel does not. Other guests are checking in, having lunch on the terrace and using the corridors while your wedding is being built, and that changes the shape of the setup day.

Access is usually through service routes rather than the front, with lifts sized for room-service trolleys rather than flight cases. Noise during setup is itself restricted, because a soundcheck at four in the afternoon is audible to everyone by the pool. And timings are tighter, because a room often has to be turned round from lunch service.

None of this is difficult. It simply rewards a supplier who has worked in operating hotels and plans the day around the hotel's day rather than assuming an empty building.

Frequently asked questions

What is different about a hotel wedding?
The people you must not disturb are asleep upstairs, and the hotel will protect them ahead of your last hour. It means the late party belongs in a contained room, and that controlling low frequencies matters more than controlling volume.

Which room should the party be in?
Not automatically the one where dinner looks best. Choose for containment and for density, because a hundred and twenty people in a room built for three hundred will feel like a thin wedding.

Can the party be outdoors?
Plan for outdoors to be the ceremony, aperitif and dinner, and the party to be inside. Catalan ordinances commonly cap outdoor music at 23:00, and sleeping guests are the more immediate factor.

Why bring a system if the hotel has one?
Because installed systems are usually built for speech and background music, not for a dance floor. Sometimes the house rig is plenty. The way to know is to ask what is installed, not to guess.
