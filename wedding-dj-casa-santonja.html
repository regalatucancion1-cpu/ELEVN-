Wedding DJ at
Casa Santonja.

Everybody who visits Casa Santonja falls for the ivy on the façade, the chapel and the hundred-year-old trees. The thing that actually decides how your night goes is underneath all of it: an old water cistern, cut into the ground, fitted out as a space for events. On a coast where the legal night starts at 22:00, a buried stone room is worth more than any view.

The cistern

The property's old water cistern has been fitted out as a space for events, and it is the feature that shapes the night here.

Across the Comunitat Valenciana the legal night begins at 22:00, and noise levels are measured at the façade of the nearest home rather than at the party, which is why the regional pattern is for the outdoor part of a wedding to run earlier and for an enclosed room to carry the later half. A stone room set into the ground is naturally suited to that second half, and it gives the property something genuinely its own: a party space with real character that is nothing like a function room.

This is general background on regional rules. Hours are set by the venue and the municipality, so confirm what applies to your own date with the venue, in writing.

Playing in the cistern

An enclosed stone room is a very good place to hold a party, and it is a space we set up for specifically rather than treating it like any other room.

Stone reflects sound, so the approach is to place the sources close to the guests rather than firing everything down the length of the room, and to keep deliberate control over the low end so that the kick and the bassline stay separate and defined. Done that way an underground room feels full and clear at a level that is comfortable to stand and talk in at the edges, which is exactly what you want at one in the morning.

A day that moves through several spaces

A wedding here can move through the chapel or the gardens for the ceremony, the grounds for the aperitif, a dining space for the meal and then the cistern for the party. Each of those wants sound that works in that space rather than sound carried over from the previous one.

  • The ceremony gets its own coverage and its own microphones, placed close to the guests, so that the celebrant, the readings and the couple are heard clearly from the back row as well as the front.
  • The aperitif across open gardens is about spreading music evenly through the space people are standing in, rather than concentrating it in one place.
  • Dinner is mostly a speech question: microphones that work for a large room, so the toasts land.
  • The party in the cistern is the one everyone pictures, and the one that benefits most from having been set up for that room in particular.

We count those spaces at the planning stage and agree who is covering each, which is the whole of it.

The shape of the evening

Across the Comunitat Valenciana the outdoor part of a wedding runs earlier than couples arriving from northern Europe expect, and an enclosed room carries the later half. That is the regional pattern rather than anything specific to one venue, and it is the shape this property is naturally set up for.

Programmed well it is invisible to the guests. The outdoor stretch reaches its own high point rather than building towards something that has to happen elsewhere, and moving down into the cistern reads as the night stepping up a gear.

Who supplies the sound

Three arrangements are all normal on this coast. The venue or its operator provides the technical side and we come as DJ only, playing on their system. You contract an AV or production company separately, often through your planner, and we work alongside them. Or there is nothing suitable for the spaces you are using, and we supply the sound and lighting ourselves, sized to the rooms rather than to the van.

For a venue of this scale the first two are the most common, and both are good outcomes. What matters is that somebody establishes which one applies, in writing, well before the week of the wedding. The failure mode at large venues is never the music. It is four suppliers each assuming one of the others was covering the ceremony.

The venue details on this page come from information Casa Santonja and its operator publish, not from a private quotation, and capacities, spaces and permitted hours change. Confirm the finishing time and the technical arrangements for your own date directly with the venue.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the old cistern matter so much?
Because it is enclosed and underground on a coast where the legal night starts at 22:00. Stone and earth mean the party can continue there when it could not continue in the gardens. Most venues here have no equivalent room.

How many guests does it hold?
The venue publishes up to 500 seated and around 600 for cocktails and exteriors, across roughly twenty thousand square metres of grounds.

What time does the music stop?
Confirm with the venue for your date. The framework is a 22:00 legal night and a licensed close of around 03:30 that a council can shorten. In practice the outdoor part ends early and the enclosed room carries the rest.

How do guests get there?
Beniarbeig is inland in the Marina Alta, a short drive from Dénia and Jávea. Valencia airport is around ninety minutes, Alicante a little more.
