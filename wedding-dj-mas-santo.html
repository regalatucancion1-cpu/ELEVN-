By the time a wedding at Mas Santo starts, thirty of your guests have been living in the house together for three days. They have eaten every meal together, they have been in the pool, and they are entirely ready. The other ninety got out of a car twenty minutes ago wearing something uncomfortable. Those are two different crowds, and the whole night turns on how quickly they become one.

Two crowds arriving in different states

Exclusive-use house weddings produce a guest list that is warm in the middle and cold at the edges, and the gap is much bigger than people expect. Three days of shared breakfasts does something to a group. The residents already have running jokes. They know where the ice is. They will dance at nine o'clock if you let them.

The day guests have none of that. They have arrived somewhere unfamiliar, they know maybe six people, and for the first two hours they will hold a drink and stand near whoever they came with.

The wrong move is to play to the warm group, because they are the ones giving you visible feedback and it feels like it is working. What actually happens is that the wedding splits: thirty people having a great time and ninety watching them. The right move is the opposite, and it is counterintuitive. Aim the first part of the evening squarely at the people who just arrived, and use the resident group as the engine rather than the audience, because they are the ones who will get up first and make the floor feel safe for everyone else.

Once those two groups merge, usually somewhere in the second hour, a house wedding goes further than a venue wedding ever does. Nobody is catching a coach and everybody is somewhere they have started to think of as theirs.

A house is wired as a house

This is the unglamorous point that saves weddings at private properties, and it applies at every villa of this kind.

A purpose-built banqueting venue has an electrical supply designed for events. A house does not, however large and however beautiful. Now add a sound system, stage and garden lighting, a catering operation with hot cabinets and fridges, and often heating or cooling, all onto a domestic supply that was specified for a family.

It rarely fails dramatically. It fails as a breaker tripping partway through the first dance, or the lights and the music going out together at the exact moment everything was working. And it is entirely preventable: establish the available supply early, spread the load deliberately across separate circuits rather than letting every supplier find the nearest socket, and where the numbers do not add up, bring a generator for the production side. Any DJ who has worked private properties will ask about this before they ask about your music taste.

Where your hours come from here

Mas Santo is a house rather than a licensed banqueting venue, which changes the legal picture in the same way it does at any villa.

In Catalonia the legal night begins at 23:00, and outdoor amplified music is commonly capped at 23:00 by the municipal ordinance, which explicitly covers private installations, so being on your own land is not an exemption. Noise limits are measured at the façade of the nearest dwelling rather than at the property boundary. And because a private house generally holds no activity licence, there is no licensed closing hour sitting behind it to fall back on.

So the question that decides your night is a geographic one: how far is the nearest permanently occupied house, and is anyone in it? In open Penedès countryside the answer is often reassuring, but it is specific to the property and you can check it yourself on a satellite map in two minutes before you ever ask.

Permissions and permitted hours are decided per property and per municipality and they change. Confirm what applies to your own date with the owner or your planner, in writing.

A hundred and twenty people in domestic rooms

The scale here is the interesting part. A hundred and twenty guests is a proper wedding, but the building is a house, which means the spaces are house-shaped: a lounge, a terrace, a garden, a pool area, rather than one hall sized for the whole party.

That has two consequences worth planning for. The crowd will spread out across several rooms and outdoor areas rather than staying in one, so music has to be present in more than one place or the party fragments into a loud room and several quiet ones. And a domestic room full of a hundred people behaves nothing like the same room empty, so anything judged at a quiet afternoon soundcheck will be wrong by ten o'clock.

The practical answer is to decide early which single space is the dance floor and commit to it, treating everywhere else as somewhere people can talk. A house wedding that tries to have two dance floors ends up with none.

Vineyards, and the sea three kilometres away

Mas Santo sits in an unusual spot. It is genuinely in the Penedès, surrounded by wine country, and it is also close enough to Sitges and the coast that guests not staying on site have real options and a Friday arrival through Barcelona airport is easy.

For a wedding weekend that combination is worth more than either thing alone. The resident group gets countryside and a pool for three days. The day guests get a short drive rather than an expedition. And the couple gets the setting without the logistics penalty that usually comes with it.

Who supplies the sound

At a private house, we do. There is no installed system to inherit, so the sound and lighting are specified for the spaces you are actually using and sized to a hundred and twenty people in domestic rooms rather than to a hall. Where you have engaged an AV or production company separately, often through a planner, we work alongside them.

For a multi-day booking it is usually worth one modest outdoor setup that stays in place for the welcome dinner and the pool day, plus a proper system for the wedding night. Those extra days are where house weddings are won, and they are the ones nobody plans until the week before.

Frequently asked questions

Why is a house wedding different?
Because thirty guests have lived there for days and ninety arrived that afternoon. They are in completely different states, and the first hours have to be aimed at the arrivals while using the residents as the engine.

Is there enough electricity?
Worth checking rather than assuming. Houses are wired for families, not for a sound system plus lighting plus a catering kitchen. Spread the load, and bring a generator if the supply does not add up.

What time does the music stop?
Confirm with the property. Catalan legal night starts at 23:00 and outdoor music is commonly capped at 23:00 including on private land. A house has no licensed closing hour behind it, so the distance to the nearest occupied home is what really decides it.

How far from Sitges and Barcelona?
A short drive to Sitges, around three kilometres to the coast, and roughly forty to fifty minutes to Barcelona.
