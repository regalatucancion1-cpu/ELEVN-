The two decisions people confuse

When a venue says it has a sound company, couples very often hear "we have to use their DJ". Those are two different things and at La Baronia only one of them is fixed.

The technical supply, meaning the PA, the lighting, the power and the person who builds and operates it, is handled by the venue's in-house company. The performer, meaning the DJ or the band, is entirely your choice, and external DJs and live groups are welcome.

Once you separate them, the arrangement stops looking like a restriction. At most Spanish venues the couple has to solve the technical side themselves, which usually means either paying a DJ to bring a full production or hiring a separate sound company and hoping the two suppliers speak to each other. Here that problem is already solved by people who work in the room every weekend.

Practical consequence for your budget: at a venue like this you are booking a DJ, not a DJ plus a rig. Ask any quote you receive to make clear which of the two it is, because the same number can mean very different things.

What the limiter actually does

There is a sound limiter here, as there is in most enclosed party spaces in Catalonia, and it is worth knowing how it works because almost nobody explains it.

It is a sealed device wired between the mains supply and the playback system. An authorised technician programmes it and it is then physically sealed or password-locked, which means neither the venue nor your DJ can legally change it. Anyone who offers to is either mistaken or planning something that puts the venue's licence at risk.

What it does:

  • Holds a threshold, often different by time of night. Limiters can be programmed with separate day and night settings and separate hours for each day of the week.
  • Cuts the power if you exceed it. Typically for a fixed period, commonly around twenty seconds. That is long enough to empty a dance floor and it takes far longer than twenty seconds to bring one back.
  • Logs everything. Levels are recorded at short intervals and retained, so the record exists whether or not anyone complains.
  • Cannot be bypassed. Disconnecting it from the mains or from its microphone stops the music, and many configurations also detect additional sound sources running in parallel. Bringing your own speaker is not a workaround.

The right response to a limiter is not to test it. It is to find out the number in advance and mix for it. A set built for a known ceiling sounds consistently good all night. A set that keeps hitting the ceiling and dropping out sounds like a technical problem, because it is one.

Three o'clock, and where that number comes from

Weddings here run to 3am, and that figure is not arbitrary.

Catalan venues licensed as a salo de banquets close at 02:30, with an additional 30 minutes on Saturdays and the eve of a public holiday. That is your three o'clock. Between 1 June and 15 September a further seasonal extension applies, so a summer Saturday has more room than an off-season Friday.

Worth knowing: these are licensed hours, not noise limits, and a town hall can reduce them for a specific venue if there have been complaints. So the number is stable but it is not guaranteed, and it is not the same on every day of the week. Ask for your own date in writing and build the running order backwards from it.

How to actually use an in-house technical team

The arrangement only pays off if somebody uses it properly, and that mostly means one conversation happening early rather than on the day.

  • Introduce your DJ or band to the sound company weeks ahead. Not the week of. This single step prevents most of what goes wrong at weddings.
  • Send a technical rider, even a short one. What you are plugging in, what you need to hear, where you need to stand. A DJ needs a booth position with sight of the floor; a band needs monitors and space. Neither is difficult if it is known in advance.
  • Agree who is on site during the party. An engineer present all night and an engineer who builds the rig and leaves are very different services. Both are legitimate; you just need to know which one you have.
  • Ask what the in-house supply covers for your format. A system specified for a DJ is not automatically adequate for a DJ plus live saxophone, guitar and drums. That gap is easy to close in advance and impossible to close at seven in the evening.

The failures we see at venues with good in-house teams are almost never equipment failures. They are conversations that never happened.
